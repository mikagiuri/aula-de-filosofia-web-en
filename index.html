Explaining a text in your own words is not copying it, nor swapping every word for a synonym, nor saying something else. Learn to tell real rewriting apart from those three traps and test yourself with passages from the great philosophers.

Sources and notice

All the material is our own work, except for the quotations and extracts from classic works, which are reproduced verbatim with their author and work indicated. They are used on a non-profit basis and solely for educational and informative purposes.

Classic texts and translations

  • Sayings: each quotation gives its author, work and location, and those that are not verbatim are marked as paraphrases, traditional formulas or attributed. The translation is not identified quotation by quotation: they are our own or adapted versions, so before quoting them in a piece of work it is advisable to check them against an edition. Those by twentieth- and twenty-first-century authors are reproduced as short quotations, with their author and work.
  • Adages: Latin and Greek texts in the public domain; our own translations and commentaries. The selection and reference numbers follow Erasmus of Rotterdam's Adagia (editions from 1500 to 1536) and the annotated English translation in the Collected Works of Erasmus (University of Toronto Press, vols 31-36).
  • Knots and film worksheets (scene, questions and misreading trap): our own work. The idea of pointing out the tensions between a respondent’s answers comes from Philosophy Experiments (philosophyexperiments.com, Jeremy Stangroom).
  • Rewriting: the passages from Plato, Aristotle, Epicurus, Hobbes, Descartes, Hume, Rousseau, Kant, Mill and Marx are our own versions based on the originals (the originals are in the public domain); the four versions of each are our own work.
  • Exploration ‘Spinoza: Philosophy as a System’ (Philosophy 1st Bach., topic 1; History of Philosophy, topic 15): the passages in the ‘Spinoza against…’ boxes (Ethics I, appendix; II, 35, scholium; III, preface; IV, 45, scholium; letter 56; Theological-Political Treatise, preface; Political Treatise I, 1) are our own versions from the Latin original; the other short sentences by Spinoza (Ethics II, 7; IV, 67; V, 42; Theological-Political Treatise, 20) are from an unidentified translation.
  • Leibniz’s Workshop: the texts are our own; the quotations and facts have been checked against C. I. Gerhardt (ed.), Die philosophischen Schriften von G. W. Leibniz, vol. VII (the ‘let us calculate’), the Monadology (§ 17), Joachim Bouvet’s letter to Leibniz of 4 November 1701 and the surviving example of the calculating machine (Hanover library).
  • Year 1 Philosophy readings: the texts by Plato, Aristotle, Descartes, Bacon, Epicurus, Pico della Mirandola, Darwin, Mill and Wilde are our own versions; Clara Campoamor’s speech to the Constituent Cortes (1 October 1931) is quoted in its original Spanish, in short extracts. The selection and the guides are our own work.
  • Saint Augustine, On the Happy Life (ch. 2): Spanish translation made from the teacher’s Basque version.
