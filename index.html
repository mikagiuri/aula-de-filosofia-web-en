Exercise

Rewriting: saying it in your own words

Explaining a text in your own words is not copying it, nor swapping every word for a synonym, nor saying something else. Learn to tell real rewriting apart from those three traps and test yourself with passages from the great philosophers.

Exercise

Sources and notice

All the material is our own work, except for the quotations and extracts from classic works, which are reproduced verbatim with their author and work indicated. They are used on a non-profit basis and solely for educational and informative purposes.

What this website records

If you click ‘Share usage data’ at the bottom of the Theory page, this website counts how it is used without knowing who you are: which sections and topics are opened, your results in quizzes, cards and the Challenge, the language, the day and the time of day (morning, afternoon or night) and, if you choose, your class group. It helps us see what works and what needs improving.

It does not record your name, your IP address or anything you write, and it does not use cookies. The teacher only sees totals per group, never what one person does, and no figure covering fewer than five visits is shown.

Nothing is sent until you switch it on. You can stop sharing whenever you like in the same place. The detailed data are deleted at the end of the school year.
